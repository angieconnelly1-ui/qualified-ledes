## What launched

On November 10, 2026, OpenAI launched **Businesses in ChatGPT** in beta. It has three parts:

- **Business Hub**, where you claim your business, correct what ChatGPT says about it, and add details like products.
- **Insights**, which show how often ChatGPT mentions your business and sends people to your site. Numbers only appear once you pass a reporting threshold.
- **Business Agents**, an assistant that answers customer questions inside ChatGPT. At launch these are limited to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses.

Verified profiles carry a blue badge. OpenAI says businesses never see users' conversations.

## Should you claim yours?

Yes, if you have 15 minutes. It is free, it lets you fix wrong details, and it gives you a first look at how often ChatGPT brings you up.

Keep expectations low on traffic. One of the first public data points, shared on the Near Media podcast, came from a jewelry store: about 46 mentions, 5 profile views and 6 website visits over 28 days. That is roughly a tenth of what the same store saw from Apple's business listings in a similar period.

## How to claim it

Verification steps vary. Reported methods include confirming you control your domain with a DNS record, confirming by email, phone or text, and an identity check for the verified badge. Have these ready:

- Access to your domain's DNS settings, or to whoever manages them
- Your business email and phone
- Accurate hours, address, services and a short description

## Everyday uses for a small team

The profile is the new part, but most of the value of ChatGPT for a small business is still in the work it saves:

- **First drafts** of emails, social posts, product descriptions and job listings, which you then edit in your own voice.
- **Customer FAQs.** Paste in the questions you get most and ask for clear, short answers to put on your site.
- **Review replies.** Get a starting point for replying to a tricky review, then make it personal.
- **Summaries.** Turn a long vendor contract, policy or report into a short list of what matters to you.
- **Planning.** Build a simple marketing calendar for the next month around your real events and promotions.

Two rules keep it safe: do not paste in customer personal data or anything confidential, and check every fact before you publish.
