## What you get

Google Ad Grants provides up to $10,000 a month in search advertising to qualifying nonprofits, at no cost. Your text ads show on Google search results, either on their own or below paid ads. The budget is use-it-or-lose-it: anything you do not spend in a month does not carry over.

For a small nonprofit, that is often far more ad budget than it could ever pay for. The catch is that the money only works if the account is set up to follow the program rules.

## Who qualifies

To get an Ad Grants account, your organization needs to:

- **Join Google for Nonprofits.** You must hold valid charity status in your country and be verified by Google's validation partner, Goodstack. Government entities, hospitals and medical groups, schools and universities are not eligible.
- **Have a high-quality website** that meets the Ad Grants website policy. Google looks for your own domain, HTTPS, a site that works on phones, and enough original content. Thin or copied pages are one of the most common reasons sites fail review.
- **Be able to meet the program policies** once you are in.

Google says most Google for Nonprofits requests are reviewed in 2 to 14 working days.

## The rules that keep the account running

Most Ad Grants trouble comes from policy, not from the application. The big ones:

1. **Mission-based keywords.** Every ad and keyword has to relate to your mission, programs and services.
2. **No single-word keywords**, apart from your own brand terms, recognized medical conditions and a few published exceptions.
3. **No overly generic keywords**, like "free videos" or "today's news", that do not show what the searcher wants.
4. **No keywords with a Quality Score of 1 or 2.** Pause them. An automated rule can do this for you.
5. **Accurate conversion tracking.** Required for accounts created since January 2018 and for any account using Smart Bidding.

Google can suspend an account that breaks program policies without notice, so a monthly check-in is worth the time.

## How to apply

1. Check eligibility and sign up for Google for Nonprofits.
2. Once verified, apply for Ad Grants from your Google for Nonprofits account.
3. After you are accepted, sign in at ads.google.com with the same email you used for Google for Nonprofits.
4. Set up conversion tracking before you launch campaigns.

## A simple first month

- Start with one campaign per program, with tightly themed ad groups.
- Write keywords as phrases people actually search, such as "free tax help in Richmond" rather than "taxes".
- Track one or two real actions: donations, volunteer signups or contact form submissions.
- Review the search terms report weekly and add negative keywords for anything off-mission.
- Pause low Quality Score keywords before they cause a policy problem.
