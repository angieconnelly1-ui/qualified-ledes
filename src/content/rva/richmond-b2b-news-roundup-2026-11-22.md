This week's Richmond B2B news was quieter than last week's, and the most useful item for revenue teams came from a follow-up story: [Richmond BizSense profiled Remission Medical](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/19/meet-remission-medical-the-telehealth-firm-that-topped-this-years-rva-25-list-with-nearly-6000-growth/), the telehealth company at the top of the RVA 25, and reported it recently hired both a chief revenue officer and a chief growth officer. A former Padilla strategist also opened his own brand consultancy, and a Midlothian contractor sold for nearly $700 million.

If you missed it, [last week's roundup](/rva/richmond-b2b-news-roundup-2026-11-15/) covered the RVA 25 list itself.

## What did Remission Medical do after topping the RVA 25?

Remission Medical added a chief revenue officer and a chief growth officer, and moved its CTO from fractional to full-time, according to BizSense's November 19 profile. Blake Wehman started the company in 2022 as a rheumatology telehealth practice, then shifted to contracting its clinicians and back-end platform to health systems, including Sentara, OrthoVirginia, Ochsner Health and Gundersen Health System. It now has 14 partnerships, up from about 8 in April, nearly 50,000 visits and a three-year average growth rate of 5,909%.

Blue Heron Capital's $5 million investment in April brought outside funding to about $6.2 million, which Wehman said buys "another year of self-funding and putting our efforts into growth."

The article doesn't name the two new executives, so I'll add them when they're public. Hiring a CRO and a growth lead at the same time, on a leadership team of six, reads like a bet on selling to many more health systems, which means long cycles and buying committees. The thing I'd watch is who owns the pipeline number and the CRM, because two new revenue leaders in a small company can end up with two definitions of a qualified lead (more on that in [why the CRM should be your single source of truth](https://angieconnelly.com/insights/crm-single-source-of-truth)).

## What's new in Richmond's agency scene this week?

Brand strategist Edward Baldwin launched Frontrunner, his own consultancy, according to [The Pitch column for November 17](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/17/the-pitch-advertising-and-marketing-news-for-11-17-26/). He previously held senior strategy roles at Joe Smith (Padilla's in-house consultancy), Deloitte and R+M. The same column reported that the University of Virginia picked Padilla to lead digital marketing for UVA Health's talent recruitment, and that Arts & Letters Creative Co. added Sephora as a client. The column sits behind the BizSense Pro paywall.

Another senior strategist going independent fits what I noticed last week with Padilla's smaller Richmond team: more of the senior marketing talent in town is available by the project. For a B2B company without a brand lead, that's a wider bench for positioning work without signing a full agency retainer.

The UVA Health account is worth a second look too. Recruitment marketing gets run a lot like demand gen, with audiences, paid media, landing pages and nurture emails, and the conversion is a job application where a B2B team would count a demo request.

## Why does Old Dominion Group's $698 million sale matter to B2B marketers?

Installed Building Products, an NYSE-listed company in Columbus, Ohio, bought Midlothian specialty contractor Old Dominion Group at an enterprise value of nearly $700 million, paid 60% in cash and 40% in stock, [BizSense reported](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/17/local-contractor-old-dominion-group-acquired-in-deal-valued-at-698m/) on November 17. ODG does insulation, asbestos abatement and similar work, with about $370 million in annual revenue, roughly 1,700 employees and 18 offices. CEO Jeff Davoud stays on, and about 75 ODG leaders and managers get equity.

It's a construction story, so it sits at the edge of what I usually cover. I'm including it for the reason Davoud gave: IBP "sought us out because of our specialized expertise and longstanding customer relationships." If you market a services company that might sell someday, those relationships carry more weight when someone can see them, which in practice means account history, owners, renewal dates and referral sources living in a CRM instead of in a founder's head.

## What's coming from Virginia lawmakers on AI in 2027?

Gov. Abigail Spanberger and General Assembly Democrats previewed their 2027 agenda on November 11, a little outside this week's window, and the item closest to marketing and HR teams is a proposal to restrict employers from using AI and machine learning to work out the lowest salary a job applicant would accept, [the Virginia Mercury reported](https://virginiamercury.com/2026/11/11/spanberger-va-democrats-pitch-medical-debt-relief-data-center-limits-for-2027/). Official bills haven't been filed yet. Drafts are due December 31, and the session convenes January 13.

The proposal is narrow, but it's about software using personal data to set a number for one person, the same idea behind the surveillance pricing bill Sen. Russet Perry prefiled, which I mentioned last week. If your team runs AI tools over candidate or customer data, now is a good time to list which ones. (I'm not a lawyer, and your counsel should have the final word on what applies to you.)
