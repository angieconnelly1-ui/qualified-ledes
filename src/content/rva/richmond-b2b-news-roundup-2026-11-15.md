This week's Richmond B2B news was mostly about growth and ownership: [Richmond BizSense published its 2026 RVA 25](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/10/rva-25-unveiled-see-which-company-topped-this-years-list-with-nearly-6000-growth/) list of the region's fastest-growing companies, a local recruiting firm sold to an out-of-town peer, and a two-person software company landed a national channel partner. I live and work in Richmond, so I'm starting a weekly roundup of local business news for B2B marketing and revenue teams, with a note on what I'd do with each item.

A small honest disclaimer: Richmond doesn't produce a SaaS funding round every week, so some weeks this will lean on local deals and events more than product launches.

## What made the 2026 RVA 25 list?

Remission Medical, founded by Blake Wehman, took the top for-profit spot, and Ronald McDonald House Richmond led the nonprofits. The list honors 35 organizations ranked by average annual revenue growth from 2023 to 2025. To qualify, a company has to be private, headquartered in the Richmond region, at least three years old and above $500,000 in 2025 revenue.

The full rankings sit behind the BizSense paywall, so I can't tell you yet how many tech firms made it. If you sell to growing local companies, it's about as clean a prospect list as Richmond gives you each year, and fast growth usually means the marketing and CRM setup that worked at $1 million starts to strain at $5 million.

## What does Cornerstone RPO's sale mean for B2B marketers?

[Hudson Talent Solutions acquired Cornerstone RPO](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/11/local-recruitment-firm-cornerstone-rpo-acquired-by-larger-out-of-town-peer/), a Richmond recruiting firm founded in 2005, in a deal that closed November 2. Hudson, a Tampa firm with more than 1,000 employees, is owned by Nasdaq-listed Star Equity Holdings. Cornerstone's roughly 50 employees and its Falmouth Street office stay, but the Cornerstone brand will be retired.

Co-founder Rodney Ashby told BizSense the combined company has "broader services and deeper capabilities," and he moves into a business development and revenue role for the Americas. The reasons given were reach into Asia-Pacific and Europe and the cost of keeping up with recruiting technology, a pressure plenty of mid-sized service firms in town will recognize.

The marketing part I'd watch is the brand retirement. When a 20-year-old name goes away, its reputation lives on in a lot of places the new owner doesn't control: old directory listings, review sites, years of backlinks and, increasingly, AI answers. AI tools will keep describing the old company for a while, and buyers who search the old name need to land somewhere sensible. I wrote about how those AI recommendations get formed in [how to rank in ChatGPT](https://angieconnelly.com/insights/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt). Merging two CRMs is the other quiet risk, because duplicate accounts and mismatched lifecycle stages make the first year of combined pipeline reporting hard to trust (more on that in [why the CRM should be your single source of truth](https://angieconnelly.com/insights/crm-single-source-of-truth)).

## How did a two-person Richmond startup land Toro as a partner?

[Turf Insight](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/10/local-golf-course-mapping-firm-takes-aim-at-national-growth-in-deal-with-turf-maintenance-giant-toro/), a drone-mapping software company founded about four years ago by David Hunsucker, is now the exclusive mapping partner for golf courses running Toro's GeoLink Spray systems. Toro customers get 10% off a service that typically runs about $10,000 a year. The deal was signed in June and announced this month, and Toro has referred nine courses since, on top of the roughly 60 Turf Insight already serves.

Hunsucker called it "a huge validation for us." For a tiny software company, a partner that already sits in the buyer's workflow can sometimes do more than a year of paid campaigns. This is one of the clearest local examples of what I call the demand surface: "The demand surface is every place a buyer can find you before they fill out a form: search results, AI answers, ads and the recommendations you never see." A referral from the company that sold you your spray system is exactly that last kind. More on that in [what is the demand surface](https://angieconnelly.com/insights/what-is-the-demand-surface).

## Why did Padilla move its Richmond office?

PR firm [Padilla moved its Richmond office](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/11/12/pr-firm-padilla-moves-richmond-office-from-manchester-to-shockoe/) from Manchester, where it had been for about 25 years, to 6,100 square feet on the second floor of Canal Crossing in Shockoe, on a five-year lease. The Richmond team is 25 people, about half its size at the time of the 2018 acquisition.

Read alongside the headcount, the move is a reminder that agency teams in Richmond, like a lot of in-house marketing teams, are getting smaller and more senior. That tracks with more local companies asking for fractional or project-based help instead of a full retainer team, which is the gap my [fractional CMO work](https://angieconnelly.com/services/fractional-cmo) is built for.

## What does Virginia's location data law mean for marketers?

Virginia's SB 338 has banned the sale of precise geolocation data under the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act since July 1, 2026, [according to Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/virginia-bans-sale-of-geolocation-data). "Precise" means data that places a person within a 1,750-foot radius. Governor Spanberger signed it April 13, and [Proskauer notes](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2026/04/15/virginia-expands-vcdpa-with-ban-on-sale-of-precise-consumer-geolocation-data/) that Virginia defines a sale as an exchange for money only, narrower than the similar bans in Maryland and Oregon.

It's older news, but worth a reminder as 2027 budgets get set. If your demand gen plan uses geofencing or location-based audiences, ask your vendors where that data comes from and whether any of it was bought. The 2027 General Assembly session convenes in mid-January, and Sen. Russet Perry has already prefiled [SB 838 on surveillance pricing](https://fastdemocracy.com/bill-search/va/2027/bills/VAB00047062/), so I'll keep an eye on whether more consumer data bills follow. (I'm not a lawyer, and your counsel should have the final word on what applies to you.)
